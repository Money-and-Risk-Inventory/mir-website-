Why engage both partners

Most households have two people. Most advisor relationships have one.

Partners often see money differently, and when only one of them has a relationship with the advisor, the other may feel no loyalty to the firm. These published findings show why it matters.

~70%
of widows change financial advisors within a year of their husband's death.
Fidelity Investments, as reported by Wealth Professional [1]
69%
of married or cohabiting couples disagreed over a financial decision in the past year.
AICPA survey, reported by Journal of Accountancy, 2021 [2]
31%
of adults with a partner say money is a major source of conflict in their relationship.
APA Stress in America survey, 2014 [3]
23%
of couples "agree to disagree" when it comes to resolving financial disagreements.
Ameriprise couples and retirement study [4]

These are third-party industry and consumer findings, not MRI results. MRI does not yet publish outcome data for Couples.

What engaging both partners does

Better conversations for the advisor. More clarity for the couple.

MRI Couples turns "we should probably talk about this" into a structured, neutral conversation.

For the advisor

  • Connect with both partners early, not only the one who usually attends meetings.
  • See disagreement before the meeting instead of discovering it during one.
  • Walk into the conversation with plain-language insights for each difference.
  • Show a clear reason for both partners to be in the room, and a deliverable they keep.

For the couple

  • Each partner answers privately, so neither has to defend their answers in front of the other.
  • The differences are framed as conversation starters, not as scores or grades.
  • Each partner can see their own results page and a Money Match report to keep.
  • The discussion moves from "who is right" to "what do we want."
